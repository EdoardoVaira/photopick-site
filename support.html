Common questions

Where can I learn how to use PhotoPick?
On your first launch, a brief animated logo leads into a few cards you swipe the way you review photos. They show comparing similar shots, choosing your own favourites and returning a pending removal to review before deletion. A privacy card explains that PhotoPick processes photos on your device and nothing is uploaded to us. Then choose where to start: your albums, or cleaning up your phone. You can skip the cards at any time. Open Settings → What PhotoPick is to see the cards again. Later, if you haven’t found a gesture yet, such as swiping a photo, double-tapping to zoom or Undo, a small hand shows it for a few seconds. You don’t have to follow it; once you use the gesture, it doesn’t come back.
What appears on Home?
Your first visit has one invitation to make an album. After that, an unfinished album takes priority with Continue. Otherwise, Home offers your monthly album near month-end, or your latest finished album. Earlier albums and new photos waiting in Swipe can appear below.
How does the monthly album work?
On the month's last two days and the next month's first two days, Home can invite you to make that month's album. For September, that's September 29 through October 2. It appears when you have Photos access, matching photos and no unfinished album, and haven’t already started that month’s album. This invitation shows the month and its emoji; your monthly photos appear when you start reviewing. Tap Make your album to start review with a name such as September 2026 🍂 already filled in. You choose what to keep; screenshots are included. With limited access, only your allowed photos are included. Starting early includes the photos available so far. Add later photos through Add photos. Once started, it uses Home’s normal Continue button. Once finished, it appears like any other album, with no separate monthly invitation. It stays saved after month-end, even if you rename it. If you allow notifications, PhotoPick sends a short reminder, such as Your September album 🍂, on the month’s last evening; if you stop opening the app, you get just that one. Tap the reminder to open that month’s album. To skip a month, touch and hold its reminder and choose Not this month; nothing leaves Photos. Turn reminders on or off in Settings → Monthly album reminder.
What is On this day?
With Photos access already allowed, Home can show photos taken on this date in earlier years. With limited access, it only uses the photos you allowed; hidden photos are excluded. If nothing matches, the section stays hidden. Tap it to browse, choose photos and start a review. Nothing is selected or kept automatically, and Home never asks for Photos access just to show it.
How are my albums and folders organised?
Albums puts In progress first, including albums inside folders. If you have more than two unfinished albums, tap Show all. Finished albums appear in a compact grid. A folder shows one of its album covers, its name and the number of albums inside; tap it to open it. Inside a folder, unfinished albums come first too. To choose its cover, open the folder’s ••• menu, tap Change cover, choose an album and confirm Use this cover. This changes only the folder’s image. Deleting a folder keeps its albums.
How do I select a whole album or a range of photos?
When creating an album, choose Photos or Albums, then tap individual photos, sweep across a row, or select a range. There is no Select all button for a new album. Select a range lets you tap a first and last photo and includes everything between them; you can adjust individual photos before tapping Review. Tap the selected count to see your batch and tap a checked photo to leave it out; Done returns to the picker. Clear empties the selection. Choosing this batch does not keep or remove any photo yet. The album gets a name automatically, which you can change during review or on the finished album. If you don’t allow Photos access, Apple’s picker is offered instead. Limited access shows only allowed photos. If a selected photo becomes unavailable, PhotoPick keeps your selection so you can retry or go back and adjust it; it does not silently leave photos out.
How can I browse a large photo library faster?
Pinch the gallery to make thumbnails smaller or larger. You can also choose Larger thumbnails or Smaller thumbnails in the ••• menu. Drag the slim date control on the right to move through a long library. Your selection stays selected while you change the view.
Do gallery thumbnails need to download?
The gallery uses available local Photos thumbnails first and prepares nearby rows as you scroll. A small preview stays visible even if a sharper version cannot load. Larger thumbnails request more detail; an internet connection may be needed if Photos has only a small preview or no local image. The cloud button retries a missing thumbnail without selecting it. Thumbnail size does not change the quality of saved originals.
Can I change the order or skip screenshots while reviewing?
Open the ••• menu and find Review order to switch between By date and Shuffled without losing your choices or moving the current photo. Skip remaining screenshots excludes only screenshots you have not decided on yet. It does not delete them or mark them removed. Screenshots you already kept or removed stay as you chose. Restore brings skipped screenshots back, including after finishing an album.
What does the heart at the top of review show?
It opens Recap: Kept and Removed photo tabs. Tap a photo for a closer look or to change its choice. Back returns to the photo you were reviewing. When you reopen an album in progress, you return to its photo review, even if you left from Recap. Add photos is in the ••• menu.
How do similar photos work?
When several photos look alike, they arrive together: the photo is a little shorter, with a strip of the group underneath. Swipe or tap to keep or remove the photo on screen, as with any photo; it leaves the strip and the next one takes its place. Slide along or tap the strip to look at the others first. Moving along the strip makes no choices. This works the same in albums and in Swipe, which checks upcoming photos as you go; PhotoPick never chooses a favourite for you.
Can I keep or remove a whole group of similar photos at once?
Yes. Press and hold Remove (✕) or Keep (the heart) until it fills: every photo still in the strip is removed or kept, including ones you haven't looked at. So you can keep the best and then hold ✕ for the rest. A tap still decides only the photo on screen. Earlier choices stay as they were, and one Undo brings the whole group back.
Why does Swipe say there are no more allowed photos?
With limited Photos access, Swipe can only show photos you have allowed PhotoPick to access. Reaching the end does not mean you have reviewed your whole library. Use Photos access in Settings to change which photos are available. Your existing choices stay saved.
Does "Remove" delete my photo?
No. In an album, Remove means the photo does not belong in that album. It stays in Apple Photos. Removing it from an album does not send it to Swipe's bin.
How do albums and Swipe work together?
Photos selected for an album are reviewed there, so Swipe skips them even before you make any choices. They stay out of Swipe after the album is finished. Screenshots you explicitly skip from album review remain available in Swipe. Assigning a photo to album review cancels any pending Swipe removal without making a Keep or Remove choice for you. If Swipe has no photos left because they are assigned to albums, it offers Open albums.
What happens if I delete a PhotoPick album?
Its photos become available in Swipe again, unless another PhotoPick album still includes them or you already made a separate Keep choice in Swipe. Old cancelled bin entries do not return. Deleting a PhotoPick album does not delete its originals from Apple Photos.
How do I put a photo into an album while swiping?
Tap the small ⌄ above the photo. A bar of circular album covers drops from the top; your photo stays in place. Tap the albums you want, then tap the photo below the bar to keep it in all of them. Tap outside both the bar and photo to close without saving. New album lets you name one, which is created when you tap the photo. Opening, cancelling, or tapping the photo with no albums selected makes no choice. This is in Swipe; while making an album you choose photos for that album only. Nothing is deleted from Apple Photos, and Undo puts everything back.
Where do removed photos go in Swipe?
Tap the bin and its count above the photo to open Removed at any time. Open a photo and tap Review again to send it back to Swipe and decide again, or touch and hold its thumbnail for the same action. You can return several without closing the bin. Delete asks for Apple's confirmation directly; cancelling leaves your photos in place. Your choices save automatically. Photos assigned to an album's review cannot be deleted through Swipe. To delete Removed photos from a finished album, use the bin on that album's own Removed tab. Deleted photos leave all album categories.
Can I delete removed photos after finishing an album?
Yes. Open the album's Removed tab and tap the bin beside Your photos to review the originals, then Delete from iPhone for Apple's confirmation. Cancelling leaves them untouched. Photos kept in any album, or kept or returned to review in Swipe, are protected. Review again returns a photo to that album's review. Album removals never fill Swipe's bin, and successful deletion removes the photos from every album's visible categories.
How do I zoom or see a photo’s details?
In Swipe and album review, double-tap or pinch the photo to zoom in place. Drag to look around; double-tap again to see the whole photo. While zoomed, dragging does not keep or remove it; the Keep and Remove buttons still work. In a similar group, the strip carries your zoom between photos. Tap the photo once to show its date, format and Edit. Tap the format on the left for full details, or tap the photo again to hide these tools.
Can I crop or rotate a photo?
In Swipe or album review, tap the photo once, then Edit at its top right. Cropping works as in Apple Photos: drag or pinch the photo under the frame, or drag a corner; Square keeps it square. You can also rotate 90° and flip. Done changes the photo in Apple Photos itself, without making a copy, and Apple Photos keeps the original: open Edit again and tap Revert, then Revert to Original, to remove every edit (Apple Photos' own Revert does the same). Your choices and albums stay as they were. Live Photos keep their motion and sound. PhotoPick can’t edit RAW, Portrait, spatial or animated photos; Apple Photos can. If the photo changes in Apple Photos while you are editing, PhotoPick asks you to reload it instead of overwriting that change.
What happens if I edit or delete a photo in Apple Photos?
For photos linked to Apple Photos, PhotoPick refreshes edits when the library changes or you return to the app. Deleted photos leave its album categories, including Removed. Restoring the same original in Photos brings back its previous choice. Reducing Photos access does not erase your choices. Edits also refresh photos in Swipe, the selection screen, comparisons and full-screen viewing. Older downloaded files stay safely stored, while the displayed photo follows Apple Photos. The updated version may need an internet connection; download it again for offline review.
Does deleting from Apple Photos free up space on my phone?
Often less than you would expect, and not straight away. With iCloud Photos set to optimise storage, most originals are not on your phone at all, so deleting them frees iCloud space rather than phone space. Deleted photos also sit in Recently Deleted for 30 days, and copies PhotoPick made for offline review stay until you remove them in Settings. After a successful deletion, PhotoPick shows Space saved for that batch when a size is available for every file. Otherwise, it shows just the photo count. The celebration also shows Total with PhotoPick, accumulated across Swipe and album deletions since the version that started counting. Earlier deletions cannot be added retrospectively. If any batch's size is unavailable, the overall total shows photos deleted instead of an incomplete space figure. If the saved history is unavailable or uncertain, only this batch is shown. Both Swipe and album cleanup celebrate successful deletion with a confetti burst, then a little confetti keeps falling until you tap Done; Reduce Motion shows a still result. Done lets you continue, and the screen points to Photos → Recently Deleted for recovery. These figures count deletion activity, even if photos are later recovered; they are based on photo file sizes, not a measurement of current free space. PhotoPick does not download originals just to obtain their sizes.
Will I lose my progress if I close the app?
No. Your choices save as you make them. Close the app whenever you like and pick up where you left off.
Why are some photos slow to appear?
If a photo is only in iCloud, iOS needs to retrieve it. PhotoPick prepares upcoming photos while you review, including a small buffer on mobile data. A slow connection can still make you wait. You can prepare an album for offline review in advance from the album's menu, or tap its download icon in Settings → App storage.
What is App storage for?
Downloading albums for offline review and managing those downloads. Small browsing previews are managed automatically. If an import was interrupted or photos remain after deleting a shared album, Settings → Saved photos lets you review them or explicitly delete their unused PhotoPick files. These may be your only copies, so save them elsewhere before deleting.
Photos stay blank while Low Power Mode is on. What can I try?
Try turning Low Power Mode off in Settings → Battery, return to PhotoPick and retry the photo. Apple can pause iCloud Photos syncing to save battery. Check your connection and try opening the same photo in Apple Photos too. You do not need to turn off iCloud Photos or delete your album. Apple's guide to paused Photos syncing explains how to check its status.
Can I add more photos to an existing album?
Open Add photos from your album or its menu. Choose photos lets you pick any photos, including screenshots, by tapping, sweeping or selecting a range. There is no Select all button. Add recent photos finds photos taken since the latest capture date already in that album, excluding photos it already contains. Check the preview, uncheck anything that belongs elsewhere, and confirm. Earlier choices and the cover stay saved; new photos start undecided. After reviewing them, use Share photos to send your updated selection. Older photos received later may fall outside the recent range: use Choose photos for those. With limited Photos access, only allowed photos can be added. This action is for your personal albums; nearby shared albums receive photos through their existing contribution flow.
Can I save the shared photos to Apple Photos?
Choose Share photos, select your photos, then use the Save action in Apple's share sheet when available. This saves files to your photo library; it does not create or update a named album and may add copies of photos already there. Your album stays saved in PhotoPick.
How do I change an album’s cover?
Open the finished album and tap the small pencil at the bottom-right of the cover, or in Albums open the album’s ••• menu and tap Change cover. Choose a kept photo, preview it, then tap Use this cover. Cancel keeps the cover you had. To change it again, choose another kept photo through the same button. From the ••• menu, an album you are still reviewing also shows the photos still to review: choosing one of those keeps it, and the button says Keep and use as cover. Undo in that album’s review brings it back. Changing the cover leaves your photo order and other choices unchanged; the ↑↓ button beside Your photos changes the order separately: drag photos into place, or open Sort photos and choose Oldest first, Newest first or Shuffle, then Done. Swipe the sheet down without Done to discard changes. Photos without a capture date stay at the end when sorting by date. PhotoPick keeps a sharp local display copy of each album cover so it stays ready between visits. A cover stored only in iCloud needs to load once before that copy is ready.
Can I keep changing an album after finishing?
Yes. Add photos from the album’s top row, or browse Kept and Removed. Open a photo and use the same Keep / Remove buttons or swipes as review. Undo at the top restores your last change. Removed photos stay outside your album; nothing is deleted from your phone by changing those choices. Share photos uses your current kept photos whenever you open it.
What if I haven’t kept any photos?
Your choices are saved, but an album needs at least one kept photo before you can finish and export it. Keep one you like, or close and come back later. Every photo in your review needs a Keep or Remove choice before finishing.
What happened to Unsure?
Review now has two choices: Keep and Remove. Photos you previously left Unsure return to review without being kept or removed for you. An album with those photos resumes review; its existing keepers and order stay as they were. In Swipe, those photos return to the deck unless they belong to an album, and stay protected from deletion until you explicitly remove them.
Can I share just some of my kept photos?
Tap Share photos, select the favourites you want to send, then tap Share. Select all shares the whole kept set. Your album and its choices stay unchanged. Photos are numbered in the order you select them; Select all appends the rest in album order. Each photo is sent once, at full quality and as it looks in Photos, with your edits; Live Photos are sent as still photos. The small previews are never used. On Wi-Fi, photos start getting ready as you select them, so sharing is quicker. If they need retrieving from iCloud, the Share button shows Downloading from iCloud and how many photos are ready. Cancel returns to your selection. The receiving app controls what it accepts and how it displays them.
Do album previews download my originals?
No. The album’s small pictures use a bounded cache of compressed previews or local Photos thumbnails. If neither is available, a photo symbol appears; open the photo to load it. iOS can clear cached previews to reclaim space. These previews never replace originals when saving or sharing.
How does sharing with friends work?
Open Friends and tap Create a shared album or Join a shared album. To join, scan your friend’s invite or paste their invitation link. Everyone needs PhotoPick; use the same Wi-Fi and keep the host and contributing phones open while photos transfer. Saved shared albums appear directly in Friends next time. Photos travel between devices, without a server. Play a round lets you pick your own favourite from the shared photos; it does not remove anyone’s photos or combine everyone’s votes.
How do I delete a shared album?
In Friends, tap the album’s ••• menu, then Delete shared album and confirm. The same option is inside an open shared album. This removes the album from your phone, without deleting photos from Apple Photos, personal albums or friends’ phones. Downloaded photos stay under Settings → Saved photos; choose Review these photos to make a personal album. No extra photo copies are created. If you created the shared album, deleting it stops sharing in that room. Friends keep photos they have already received.

Reporting a problem

The most useful report says what you expected, what happened instead, and how many photos were involved. Screenshots or a screen recording are ideal. You will not be asked to send any of your photographs.
