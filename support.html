Common questions

Where can I learn how to use PhotoPick?
The updated introduction appears once, including for existing users. You can skip it; your albums and choices stay as they are. Open Settings → What PhotoPick is to see how to choose photos, review them and save your album. How to review photos explains the controls. Take a practice round lets you try sample photos without choosing your own. Practice is optional and resets when you close it.
What appears on Home?
Your first visit has one invitation to make an album. After that, an unfinished album takes priority with Continue. Otherwise, Home offers your monthly album near month-end, or your latest finished album. Earlier albums and new photos waiting in Swipe can appear below.
How does the monthly album work?
On the month's last two days and the next month's first two days, Home can invite you to make that month's album. For September, that's September 29 through October 2. It appears when you have Photos access, matching photos and no unfinished album, and haven’t already started that month’s album. This invitation shows the month and its emoji; your monthly photos appear when you start reviewing. Tap Make your album to start review with a name such as September 2026 🍂 already filled in. You choose what to keep; screenshots are included. With limited access, only your allowed photos are included. Starting early includes the photos available so far. Add later photos through Add photos. Once started, it uses Home’s normal Continue button. Once finished, it appears like any other album, with no separate monthly invitation. It stays saved after month-end, even if you rename it. If you allow notifications, PhotoPick sends a short reminder, such as Your September album 🍂, on the last evening of each month; tap the reminder to open that month’s album. The app icon counts monthly albums still waiting. Tap Not this month to skip one; nothing leaves Photos. Turn reminders on or off in Settings → Monthly album reminder.
What is On this day?
With Photos access already allowed, Home can show photos taken on this date in earlier years. With limited access, it only uses the photos you allowed; hidden photos are excluded. If nothing matches, the section stays hidden. Tap it to browse, choose photos and start a review. Nothing is selected or kept automatically, and Home never asks for Photos access just to show it.
How do I select a whole album or a range of photos?
When creating an album in the updated selection screen, choose Photos or Albums. Select all includes the photos in the view you are browsing. Select a range lets you tap a first and last photo and includes everything between them; you can adjust individual photos before tapping Review. The album gets a name automatically, which you can change during review or on the finished album. If you don’t allow Photos access, Apple’s picker is offered instead. Limited access shows only allowed photos. If a selected photo becomes unavailable, PhotoPick keeps your selection so you can retry or go back and adjust it; it does not silently leave photos out.
How can I browse a large photo library faster?
Pinch the gallery to make thumbnails smaller or larger. You can also choose Larger thumbnails or Smaller thumbnails in the ••• menu. Drag the slim date control on the right to move through a long library. Your selection stays selected while you change the view.
Do gallery thumbnails need to download?
The gallery uses available local Photos thumbnails first and prepares nearby rows as you scroll. A small preview stays visible even if a sharper version cannot load. Larger thumbnails request more detail; an internet connection may be needed if Photos has only a small preview or no local image. The cloud button retries a missing thumbnail without selecting it. Thumbnail size does not change the quality of saved originals.
Can I change the order or skip screenshots while reviewing?
Open the ••• menu and find Review order to switch between By date and Shuffled without losing your choices or moving the current photo. Skip remaining screenshots excludes only screenshots you have not decided on yet. It does not delete them or mark them removed. Screenshots you already kept, removed or left unsure stay as you chose. Restore brings skipped screenshots back, including after finishing an album. Deleting photos you marked Remove from your phone waits until the album is finished: it is a separate, confirmed screen reached from the album's Removed photos.
What does the heart at the top of review show?
It opens Recap: Kept, Unsure and Removed photo tabs. Tap a photo for a closer look or to change its choice. Back returns to the photo you were reviewing. When you reopen an album in progress, you return to its photo review, even if you left from Recap. Add photos is in the ••• menu.
Can I compare similar photos in Swipe?
In builds with Swipe similarity, related photos open the same comparison view as album review. Browse them, choose Keep, Remove or Unsure, and use Undo if you change your mind. Browsing alone makes no choices. PhotoPick checks upcoming photos as you swipe; it does not scan your whole library before starting or choose a favourite for you.
Does "Remove" delete my photo?
No. Remove excludes a photo from the album you're making. Your photo stays in Apple Photos exactly as it was. Deleting from your library is a separate screen that shows you the exact photos first and asks for Apple's confirmation. Albums and Swipe use the same Removed screen. Its gallery uses small previews; tap a photo to inspect and zoom in. Tap Review again to send it back to your review, ready for a new choice. You can also touch and hold a thumbnail and choose Review again. You can return several before tapping Done.
Where do removed photos go in Swipe?
Tap the bin and its count above the photo to open Removed at any time. Open a photo and tap Review again to send it back to Swipe and decide again. You can return several without closing the bin. Delete asks for Apple's confirmation directly; cancelling leaves your photos in place. Your choices save automatically, so you can leave Swipe whenever you like. Photos protected by an album stay safe.
What happens if I edit or delete a photo in Apple Photos?
For photos linked to Apple Photos, PhotoPick refreshes edits when the library changes or you return to the app. Deleted photos leave its album categories, including Removed. Restoring the same original in Photos brings back its previous choice. Reducing Photos access does not erase your choices. An intentionally downloaded copy stays safe: open it full-screen and tap Open updated photo to replace it with the edited version.
Does deleting from Apple Photos free up space on my phone?
Often less than you would expect, and not straight away. With iCloud Photos set to optimise storage, most originals are not on your phone at all, so deleting them frees iCloud space rather than phone space. Deleted photos also sit in Recently Deleted for 30 days, and copies PhotoPick made for offline review stay until you remove them in Settings. After a successful deletion, PhotoPick shows the photo count and the total file size when a size is available for every file. Otherwise, it shows just the photo count. The completion screen points to Photos → Recently Deleted for recovery. The total is not a measurement of free space on your phone. PhotoPick does not download originals just to obtain their sizes.
Will I lose my progress if I close the app?
No. Your choices save as you make them. Close the app whenever you like and pick up where you left off. The sample practice round resets when closed.
Why are some photos slow to appear?
If a photo is only in iCloud, iOS needs to retrieve it. PhotoPick prepares upcoming photos while you review, including a small buffer on mobile data. A slow connection can still make you wait. You can prepare an album for offline review in advance from the album's menu, or tap its download icon in Settings → App storage.
Photos stay blank while Low Power Mode is on. What can I try?
Try turning Low Power Mode off in Settings → Battery, return to PhotoPick and retry the photo. Apple can pause iCloud Photos syncing to save battery. Check your connection and try opening the same photo in Apple Photos too. You do not need to turn off iCloud Photos or delete your album. Apple's guide to paused Photos syncing explains how to check its status.
Can I add more photos to an existing album?
Open Add photos from your album or its menu. Choose photos lets you pick any photos, including screenshots. Add recent photos finds photos taken since the latest capture date already in that album, excluding photos it already contains. Check the preview, uncheck anything that belongs elsewhere, and confirm. Earlier choices and the cover stay saved; new photos start undecided. Save album to Photos again after reviewing them to update the same exported album. Older photos received later may fall outside the recent range: use Choose photos for those. With limited Photos access, only allowed photos can be added. This action is for your personal albums; nearby shared albums receive photos through their existing contribution flow.
Does saving an album reduce my photo quality?
No. Saving to Apple Photos reuses your original photos — nothing is re-encoded or duplicated. The album simply points at the photos you already have.
Can I keep changing an album after finishing?
Yes. Add photos from the album’s top row, or browse Kept, Unsure and Removed. Open a photo and use the same Keep / Remove / Unsure buttons or swipes as review. Undo at the top restores your last change. Unsure and Removed photos stay outside your album; nothing is deleted from your phone by changing those choices. Save album to Photos again to update the exported album.
What if I haven’t kept any photos?
Your choices are saved, but an album needs at least one kept photo before you can finish and export it. Keep one you like, or close and come back later. Other Unsure photos can stay Unsure; they won’t be included in the export.
Can I share just some of my kept photos?
Tap Share photos, select the favourites you want to send, then tap Share. Select all shares the whole kept set. Your album and its choices stay unchanged. Sharing uses original files in your album’s order; an internet connection may be needed to retrieve originals from iCloud. The receiving app controls what it accepts and how it displays them.
Do album previews download my originals?
No. The album’s small pictures use a bounded cache of compressed previews or local Photos thumbnails. If neither is available, a photo symbol appears; open the photo to load it. iOS can clear cached previews to reclaim space. These previews never replace originals when saving or sharing.
How does sharing with friends work?
Open Friends and tap Create a shared album or Join an album. To join, scan your friend’s invite or paste their invitation link. Everyone needs PhotoPick; use the same Wi-Fi and keep the host and contributing phones open while photos transfer. Saved shared albums appear directly in Friends next time. Photos travel between devices, without a server. Play a round lets you pick your own favourite from the shared photos; it does not remove anyone’s photos or combine everyone’s votes.
How do I delete a shared album?
In Friends, tap the album’s ••• menu, then Delete shared album and confirm. The same option is inside an open shared album. This removes the album from your phone, without deleting photos from Apple Photos, personal albums or friends’ phones. Downloaded photos stay under Settings → App storage → Saved shared photos; choose Review these photos to make a personal album. No extra photo copies are created. If you created the shared album, deleting it stops sharing in that room. Friends keep photos they have already received.

Reporting a problem

The most useful report says what you expected, what happened instead, and how many photos were involved. Screenshots or a screen recording are ideal. You will not be asked to send any of your photographs.
